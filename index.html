Fire, crickets, cicadas and wind

These are real recordings, not synthesised. The fire is ten minutes of one continuous fireplace recording. The app plays passages of different lengths from different parts of each recording and blends them with slow crossfades, cutting at quiet moments between crackles. That makes repeats hard to notice, but it is still the same recordings coming round again, not a new fire. Nothing is pitch-shifted or sped up.

Session timer and listening device

Binaural beats only work through headphones. On speakers, isochronic tones work best.

Brainwave tones

You can layer several binaural beats and several isochronic tones at once. Each layer has its own beat, carrier tone, volume, drift and fade in. Isochronic layers also have a pulse softness control, from a hard, crisp pulse to a very soft swell. Each layer can also follow its own beat journey: it starts at its beat frequency, goes down to a deeper rate, holds there, then comes back up to a finishing rate, all within the session length you choose. The app works out the timing: half of the time not spent holding is used going down, half coming back up. Journeys need a set session length. These are changes in sound only; they cannot guarantee any particular state of mind.

Tone type chooses which kind of layers you hear. Adding a layer of the other kind switches this to Both together.

Binaural beat layers

Each binaural layer plays one tone in each ear. Use headphones.

Isochronic tone layers

Each isochronic layer is a tone that pulses on and off. They work on speakers too.

Solfeggio tones

Soft, steady tones that slowly swell. You can layer up to 4, each with its own frequency, volume, swell and position. They are generated by this page. The traditional meanings of solfeggio frequencies are not proven science.

Singing bowls

Up to 4 singing bowls, each struck on its own schedule. Each can be the real recorded Tibetan bowl, a synthesised bowl, or both taking turns. The real recording changes pitch by playing faster or slower, so very low or high pitches change how long it rings. The synthesised bowl is made by this page.

Breathing guide

A soft tone rises while you breathe in and falls while you breathe out. Six breaths a minute is a well-known calming pace.

Slow movement for speakers

Optional. The whole mix slowly gets a little quieter and softer, then returns, over and over, like breathing. It never gets louder than your normal mix, and nothing jumps suddenly. It works on a phone speaker; no headphones needed. This is a listening effect only. It doesn't change your brain state and isn't a treatment.

Session stages

Optional. Save your current mix as a stage, then add more stages with different mixes and lengths. When stages are switched on, a session moves through them in order by itself, gliding smoothly from one mix to the next. After the last stage, the last mix keeps playing until you stop. For a beat that goes down, holds and comes back up within one session, use the Beat plan on each brainwave layer instead. The session length setting is ignored while stages are on. Stop works at any time.

If the iPhone pauses the page, for example when the screen locks, the stages pause too and carry on from the same point when sound resumes.

Starting points

Optional. Each one sets the tones, bowls and nature volumes in one go. Every control above can still be changed afterwards. Your settings are saved on this phone automatically.

Other apps, screen lock and saving audio

Mix with other audio: plays alongside other apps without pausing them, but the iPhone's silent switch mutes it, and it usually stops when the screen locks. Keep playing when the screen locks: plays like a music app, even in silent mode, and tries to keep going when locked, but it pauses other apps' audio. An iPhone can't do both from a web page. If you change this during a session, the session restarts.

Lock-screen track

If live sound still stops when the screen locks, press Make lock-screen track. It records about three minutes of your current mix as a seamless loop and plays it through a normal audio player. The breathing voice and session timer don't work with it, and if you change settings afterwards you'll need to make a new track.

Save a session as an audio file

This makes an audio file of your current settings, with a fade in and a fade out, which you can save to the Files app and play in any audio app. Some audiobook and podcast apps can play it alongside other audio, and it keeps playing when the screen locks. The file is made on your phone, so it takes a minute or two. The mix is three to four minutes of sound, blended seamlessly and repeated for the full length.

Help and tips

  • Ten minutes of fire and several minutes each of crickets, cicadas and wind download when the page opens, about 17 megabytes in all. On mobile data that can take a minute; the status message tells you when they are ready.
  • Sound only starts after you press Start session. iPhones need a tap before a page can make sound.
  • If you hear nothing on an iPhone, check the setting called How this page plays sound. In Mix with other audio, the silent switch mutes the page, so turn silent mode off. In Keep playing when the screen locks, it plays even in silent mode on recent iPhones.
  • To listen with an audiobook: choose Mix with other audio, start your audiobook first, then start a session here. Or save a session as an audio file and play it in an app that mixes audio.
  • Use headphones for binaural beats. Each ear gets a slightly different tone, and your brain hears the difference as a slow beat. Isochronic tones pulse on and off, so they work on speakers too.
  • Low carrier tones, around 60 to 100 hertz, sound deep and warm on headphones and good speakers. A phone speaker can't play them well, so choose Phone speaker under Listening on.
  • Bass warmth adds a gentle boost to the low end for good speakers. A built-in limiter keeps it from distorting.
  • Brainwave bands: delta, 0.5 to 4 hertz, deep sleep. Theta, 4 to 8, meditation and dreaminess. Alpha, 8 to 13, relaxed calm. Low beta, 13 to 20, focus and alertness. Gamma, around 40.
  • Keep the volume comfortable and low. Gentle is more relaxing than loud.
  • Steppers: every slider has Lower and Raise buttons beside it. With VoiceOver, you can also swipe up or down on a slider to change it.
  • On a phone speaker, choose Phone speaker under Listening on. That keeps every sound centred, because side-to-side movement only means something on headphones.
  • To open it like an app, in Safari tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
